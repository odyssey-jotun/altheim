order: 10
session: 5
title: "The Frozen Level"
---
Rested, the company loaded the mine carts and rode down to the next level.

A freezing wind met them at the bottom, blowing from one direction only. Rime coated the tracks and walls. Cracks in the rock gave off a pale blue glow from somewhere behind the stone, and the farther the company went, the harder the wind blew, until snow was catching in their hair. Jack said it felt like Ten Towns.

A faint warm glow came from the other way, with no wind behind it. Jack had the company look there first.

A side passage held crates of lantern parts and barrel after barrel of kerosene, about forty-five pounds apiece. Jack identified it the hard way, by tasting it.

Seamus stopped at one of the glowing cracks. The frost at its edge melted at a touch, and the meltwater did not drip. It gathered itself into a little sphere on the stone, rippled, and popped. Seamus worked an eldritch dagger into the crack, the water trailed along the blade and balled up again, and as it burst there was a faint echo of laughter. Female laughter, and familiar. Up close, the water held extraordinarily fine glowing shards. Valith's flame melted the frost around the crack and did nothing to the glow. Seamus caught a quarter of a canteen of the stuff. Looking down its mouth, the water seemed to pulse.

The warm glow was a lantern in an abandoned camp: a table, a backpack, a miner's log, a cave-in behind a barricade, and crates of the same quartz the company had harvested above. The log, in Dwarvish, which Jack and Wonnelly could both read, said this vein was coming along even after the cave-in, but could not compare to the eastern cavern of this level.

Wonnelly took the pitons from the backpack. The lantern burned pinkish amber with no oil in it, gave off no smoke, and almost no heat. Seamus had suspected a fairy. There was none, but there was magic in it. Valith put her hand through the flame and called it an abomination. Seamus kept it, and Cocoa wore it on a belt so the whole company could see thirty feet ahead.

The quartz would sell in Caldmere, though Jorik had said anything in the mine remained the mining company's property. Pockets were filled anyway.

Then the company went back for the kerosene. Two barrels went into each cart and four into Cocoa's, and Valith was told to keep her hands in her pockets. If something ahead needed burning, they would send a cart down the tracks and let her light it.

Onward, into the wind.
