On the way home to Caldmere the company stopped at Frostlass Pastures for another round of the S-rank cream, the milk that leaves a mustache and a little heroic inspiration behind. Bruno, who drives the dairy's wagon, was still missing. His sister had not seen him in days.

Back on the main road, the company found wagon tracks turning down an old dirt road nobody used anymore.

At the end of it stood a camp on a plateau: cabins, a fenced pen, a well, a watchtower, and a Frostlass wagon, busted and spattered with mud, dragged across the road as a barricade. A second wagon blocked the other way.

A halfling came out from behind the barricade, weathered, somewhere around fifty. He locked eyes with Wonnelly.

"Shadowleaf. Of all the people they could have sent."

Tobin Rook had been Wonnelly's lieutenant in the city guard, and they had worked well together. Now he wore the silver insignia of the Guild, and as he came closer Wonnelly could see it was a knockoff.

He had never heard of Bruno. The wagons were here when he arrived. His colleagues were going about their business, and no, Wonnelly would not know them. The cabin was off limits: there was sickness inside, and it was catching. He was here on behalf of the Adventurers' Guild in Caldmere, and no, they had not given him a liaison.

Seamus asked, quietly, what the Guild's bylaws said about people who impersonate its members. A year in the dungeon at the least, if they are taken alive, and more depending on what they did with the insignia.

Jack walked around the wagon and stood over Tobin, all six foot seven of Jack. Tobin did not tell the truth. He yelled "Hold the road!" and archers came up on the tower.

Two big men with cudgels came first. One clubbed Cocoa and grabbed hold. The other found Jack, whose new armor turned the first blow, then landed a natural twenty and pinned Jack too. Cocoa's oath sent part of that hit back at the man as radiant light.

Pinned, Jack drove a sword up into the man's gut and twisted it, and he let go and fell. Cocoa spent the milk's inspiration on a second swing, took the other man's hand off, and smote him in the chest while he stared at the stump.

Wonnelly put two crossbow bolts into the man in the doorway. Valith burned the tower down. The fireball pulled the air back in behind it, and when it cleared there was a crater where the tower had stood and no way left to run.

Tobin ducked behind the fence of the pen and put a bolt into Valith. Seamus misty stepped across the yard and opened a man's shoulder with a glaive. Jack spent two strokes of luck and an action surge on the camp's one real swordsman, who had clearly done this before. Cocoa finished him with a natural twenty. The arrow Cocoa had caught out of the air at the start of the fight went into the swordsman's eye, and the greatsword went through his neck and into the wooden doorframe behind him.

"Shadowleaf, I surrender."

Bruno was in the cellar under the main cabin, gagged, hands tied, one eye blackened from trying to beat his way out. It had taken both of the big men to put him there. Cocoa went down to get him alone, and let Bruno believe Cocoa had come for him alone.

Bruno told it this way. Men in Guild insignias stopped his wagon and told him there was a new toll station on the road. He meant to pay and go. Then he saw one of the smaller ones shoving people into a cellar outside, and he asked if they were really from the Guild. Someone hit him from behind. His bull, Onion, slept through all of it behind the wagon.

As he finished, the outside cellar door opened. A frightened couple in their thirties came up with their child, and behind them came Company 214: Vera's squad. They had come in through the catacombs under the camp on a different contract, The Empty House, to rescue this family. The couple pointed at Tobin. He was the one who tied them up.

Vera offered to turn in the company's contract for them. Seamus offered to split her bounty, since the company had done half of Vera's work. Neither offer was taken. Reginald would sort it out. Torque rebuilt a wagon from the wreckage, Lily the healer smiled and waved, Brent the rogue said "Smell you later," and Company 214 left for Caldmere with their blink dog. Seamus told Vera to make sure the company was in her report.

The main cabin held a long table, a hearth, a bed, and a desk with a ledger on it: every traveler who had paid the toll, and what each one paid. No house leaders, but more than one name from the nobility. Beside the desk sat a locked chest. Wonnelly's lockpick snapped off in it on a natural one, so Cocoa took the lid off instead. Inside were 521 gold pieces, and the ledger's sums matched almost to the coin.

There was a proposal to burn the ledger. There was a proposal to burn Tobin. There was a plan to give the gold to Bruno, let the dairy reinvest it, and drink the returns as better milk. Jack and Seamus talked the company out of all of it. If they skimmed, the Guild would never trust them with gold again, and a company that turned down 521 gold for its good name would be noticed. Everything went back to Caldmere.

Before they left, Bruno gave them Bova's Bell. It is a brass cowbell of about five pounds with the dairy's mark on it, the kind hung on prize cattle so they can be found in a blizzard, and it has passed down through the family for generations. It holds an effigy of Bova, the first heifer to give the dairy's famous cream.

Once a day it can be rung. Every creature within sixty feet who hears it, the ringer included, must keep its wits or spend a minute fixed on a phantom cow grazing nearby. If the ringer has the cream's inspiration, the phantom becomes a spectral Bova, solid enough to hide behind, glowing dimly, for ten minutes.

Wonnelly carries it. "Bova is a bit of a national treasure at our house," Bruno said. "Take care of her."
