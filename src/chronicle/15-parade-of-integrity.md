Tobin walked into Caldmere tied up behind Seamus's spectral steed, which carried the chest. People stopped in the street. The guards at the gate stared at the insignias.

At the Guild, Lydia was busy with Vera, who was finishing her paperwork. Seamus asked whether the company's names were in it. They were not.

Upstairs, the company set the gold, the ledger, and Tobin in front of Reginald. The contract for this one, False Silver, was posted while they were away, so they took it now. Reginald read down the ledger and knew many of the names. Lady McCarthy was among them.

Jack showed him the Winter Heart. He asked Jack to put it away.

He submitted both contracts. The company asked whether all this might be worth a rank. That, Reginald said, was not his decision.

Their suite smelled of sulfur. Two eggshells lay by the fireplace, yellow goo was spackled up the walls and dripping from the ceiling, and Guzzard lay asleep in the middle of the king bed with one arm hanging off and a round, full belly. He had eaten one of Blind Fang's eggs.

Seamus cast thaumaturgy and boomed, "Rise, maggot."

Guzzard would clean if he got something shiny, or his doll back. Not the doll. Something shiny. He tied on a little doily apron and a doily cap, took up a feather duster, and went to work. When the company passed the door later they heard music, rustling, and something break.

At the Prism Palace the penguins were working the floor as before, sliding on their bellies when they were in a hurry. Jenkins examined the Heart without taking it out of its wrapping. Master Aurelius was away. Rumors abound, Jenkins said.

The thing was primordial, he said, older than Auril and her followers, and the name was right: the Winter Heart, the essence of winter itself. In the right hands it could be forged into almost anything, and it was likely one of a kind. He offered 7,000 gold, and more if the master came back.

The company called it a lowball. Valith wondered aloud how the penguins would taste roasted. Jenkins took his leave for lunch.

Outside, Jack offered to sell it and take only a thousand. The rest of the company wanted weapons. The Heart is not for sale.

At Bimble & Sons a giant grapevine owl now hung from the ceiling. Bimble named Jack's armor before anyone said a word: pearlite, from the artisan in Keldrun. Bimble's clue is true.

The Winter Heart he could not name. But he unrolled a map across the counter and traced a finger into the northeast, into the mountains called the Old Teeth. Up there, at Cairn Mordun, the rumor goes, is an undead smith who forges primordial relics, his soul bound to the anvil. The hammer can be heard all through the Old Teeth, day and night. Everybody knows the place is haunted. Nobody goes.

Jack gave Bimble a crystal from the Keldrun mine to hand down to his son one day.

Back upstairs, Reginald had results. Coldiron Mine paid its base reward of 400 gold. The company did what the contract asked, he said, where on the goblin job they had gone well past it. False Silver was posted at 180. For the thief, the ledger, and the gold, the Guild raised it to 300. Seven hundred gold, less Reginald's small cut. He has to keep the lights on.

Seamus said he was tired of three-digit contracts. Valith said the company was headed for the Old Teeth. Reginald knew about Cairn Mordun, the worst-haunted place in the kingdom, and would not advise it at the company's level. He did not want to lose his best investment. He would look into the region, and into anyone who knows it. In the meantime he had three contracts.

Ember Dispatch, 350 gold. A House Ember courier carrying letters from the Iron Ascent to Caldmere is missing. His horse came home without him.

The Fire at Zahara's Glassworks, 300 gold, here in town. The magical kiln is acting up, the workers were evacuated, and an apprentice who went back inside has not come out.

The Quiet Exchange, 500 gold. A covenant contract, which costs the one who posts it a great deal. To read it, everyone within fifteen feet gives a drop of blood, and the blood becomes the words. Whoever reads it is bound to keep it secret until it is closed, canceled, or timed out. All Reginald knew was that it came from Lord Alistair Vale himself, head of House Dawn.

The company chose the Glassworks first and the Quiet Exchange after. Then they voted, three to two, to read the covenant now. The two who voted no went to check on Guzzard.

Reginald had one more thing to say before the blood. Anyone who knowingly reveals what the contract says will trigger its defense before the words leave their mouth, and be taken away to a cell on another plane.

The session ended there.
